# The build plan: four parts

Every lab project has the same four parts. They keep the work tied to the person's real
job, and they are what the person brings to the group session. Collect them through
conversation. Never hand the person this list as a form.

## 1. The before picture

How the task gets done today, before any AI is involved.

- How do you do this now?
- How long does it take each time?
- How often does it come up?
- What part do you dread or put off?

Why it matters: without a before picture, nobody can say whether the new way is better.
A rough answer is enough. "About three hours every month" is a before picture.

## 2. What better means

One sentence, agreed before building, in the person's own words.

Good answers are specific and theirs:

- "Under an hour, and it still sounds like us."
- "My director stops rewriting the first page."
- "I stop missing the volunteers who signed up late."

If the answer is vague ("more efficient"), ask what they would notice on an ordinary
Tuesday if it were working.

Why it matters: it gives both of you something to check the result against, and it
stops the project from drifting into whatever is easy to build.

## 3. The smallest useful piece

The first thing built is one piece the person can use today, not the whole task.

- One section of the grant report, not the report.
- One week of the schedule, not the season.
- One thank-you letter that sounds right, not the mail merge.

Choose the piece that is either the most painful or the easiest to check. Once it works,
the next piece is the one beside it.

Why it matters: a small result arrives in minutes, the person can judge it, and what
they learn from judging it shapes everything after.

## 4. The first people

Who else touches this work, and who will use the new way first.

Raise this only once something works for the person. Then:

- Who else does this task, or reads what comes out of it?
- Who would you show it to first, and what would they need to trust it?
- What would stop a colleague from using it?

Why it matters: a tool one person uses quietly helps one person. Most lab projects are
worth more when a second and third person pick them up, and the person who built it is
the right one to bring them in.

## Checking back

When the piece is in use, return to parts 1 and 2. How long does it take now? Did
"better" happen? An honest "not yet" is a useful answer and a good thing to bring to the
group.
